# BnBDetector for Agents and Relocation Teams

Agents, relocation consultants, and property managers get asked a question listings rarely answer: will this building feel residential once the client moves in? BnBDetector is a fast way to put a BnBIndex and a nearby Airbnb count next to each address you are about to recommend.

Use it as screening, then say what it is. The report shows active Airbnb listings detected around the address, about an 80-meter radius, summarized from 0 to 100. It is not an appraisal, not a legal opinion, and not a promise that the client will sleep well. The Terms of Service say reports are informational and provided as-is: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/tos

## How to put it in a search

1. Shortlist for the client’s real constraints first.
2. Run each finalist address from your BnBDetector account. One new address, one report.
3. Send the client the address, the date, the BnBIndex, the category, and the nearby count. Avoid “this building is illegal” or “apartment 12 is an Airbnb.”
4. Prefer 0–30 when the brief is a quiet long stay. Flag 31–58 as mixed. Flag 59–100 as heavy Airbnb pressure and keep it on the list only if the client wants that trade for location or price.
5. ReCheck the property they are ready to take, on Max or Ultra, so the note in the file matches the week they sign.
6. Tell them to read the lease or building rules, and to visit. Your report does not close those loops.

Max (25 reports a month) fits a steady book of client searches and includes history plus city and country browsing, which is how a desk compares this month’s addresses later. Ultra (60 a month) is the published ceiling for a single account. Both figures are the prices and allowances on https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing and can change there.

If the desk needs more than 60 checks a month, several people with their own seats, or an integration into another tool, use the contact form. This documentation does not describe a public API. https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/contact

## How to talk about privacy with clients

Checks stay on the account that ran them. BnBDetector does not notify the landlord or the other agency that an address was screened. Do not share your login to “let the client click around.” If the client should hold the report themselves, they can run it on their own account, or you can quote the dated result you already produced.

Do not resell raw access to the account. The Terms restrict transferring account access.

## Landlords and managers watching their own building

Some owners run their own address because they want to know when nearby listing activity climbs, before residents complain. That is a fair use of reCheck: same address, once a month on Max or Ultra, a dated score you can compare with the last one.

A rising score is a reason to review house rules and talk to residents. It is not, alone, an enforcement file. Pair it with the lease, the house rules, and whatever registration system your city actually runs.

## What good client notes look like

> Checked 8 March. 120 Example Street. BnBIndex 22, Residential Core, 9 active Airbnb listings detected nearby. Lower Airbnb pressure than 15 Other Street (BnBIndex 68 the same day). Client still needs to confirm the building’s minimum-stay rule.

That note can be defended. A note that says the city has certified the building cannot.
