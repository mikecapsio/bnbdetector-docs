# BnBDetector vs Searching Airbnb Yourself

Searching Airbnb by hand is free, and it shows you photos and reviews. BnBDetector turns nearby Airbnb activity at a street address into a BnBIndex from 0 to 100 and a listing count, usually in under 10 seconds. Most people who are about to sign should screen with BnBDetector and open Airbnb only when a building still needs a closer look.

## Short answer

Use a manual Airbnb search when you want to see listing photos, guest reviews, and the vague map pin for a place you already suspect.

Use BnBDetector when you have several real addresses and you need the same measure on each of them before you rent, buy, or renew.

Use both when a score is in Mixed Community or Airbnb Dominated and you are still willing to live there. The score tells you the pressure. The listings show you what that pressure looks like.

## What each one gives you

### BnBDetector

- An address in, a report out.
- Active Airbnb listings counted nearby, about an 80-meter radius in the current product.
- A 0–100 BnBIndex with four bands: Local Haven (0–9), Residential Core (10–30), Mixed Community (31–58), Airbnb Dominated (59–100).
- The same report on every plan, so a shortlist is comparable.
- A private account. The building is not notified that you checked.
- A saved result, and on Max and Ultra a free reCheck once per address each month.

### Searching Airbnb yourself

- Photos, titles, prices, reviews, and house rules for listings you can find.
- A map that is deliberately imprecise until a stay is booked.
- No single score you can put next to five other buildings.
- No account with the landlord, and no fee.
- Easy to stop too early and call the block “fine” because the first pins looked residential.

## Why the manual search is hard to trust alone

Airbnb’s map is designed to hide the exact home. You are matching a circle, a facade crop, and a description that says “quiet street in the center.” One building’s listings may sit under slightly different pins. A fifteen-minute browse can miss half of them, and it will miss a home that is listed on another site entirely. BnBDetector has the second limit too: the report is Airbnb activity, not every platform. It is built to count that Airbnb activity consistently, which a manual browse does not do.

The manual search also has no memory. Next month you will not remember whether you saw six pins or sixteen. A saved BnBIndex and a count are easier to reCheck.

## What BnBDetector will not show you

It will not show the kitchen, the reviews, or the host’s tone. It will not hand you the listing URL for apartment 4B. When those details matter, open Airbnb after the score, not instead of it.

It will also not prove the pin is inside the property line. “Nearby” includes the immediate neighbors. Read the count as area pressure. Confirm a specific flat with your eyes.

## A sequence that wastes less time

1. Put only real candidate addresses into BnBDetector.
2. Set aside Airbnb Dominated buildings unless location forces the issue.
3. For a mixed or high score you might still accept, search Airbnb around that pin and look at photos and reviews.
4. Visit.
5. ReCheck before you pay, on a plan that includes it.

## Bottom line

Searching Airbnb answers “what do the listings I can find look like?” BnBDetector answers “how much Airbnb activity is detected around this address, on a scale I can compare?”

Screen with the score. Investigate with the map.
