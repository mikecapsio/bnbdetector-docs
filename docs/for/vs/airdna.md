# BnBDetector vs AirDNA

BnBDetector and AirDNA both look at short-term rental data, and they are built for opposite decisions. BnBDetector tells a future resident how much Airbnb activity sits around an address. AirDNA tells a host or investor how a short-term rental market performs, and what a property might earn.

If you are signing a lease, start with BnBDetector. If you are underwriting a rental you intend to operate, start with AirDNA, and still check whether local rules allow it.

## Short answer

Choose BnBDetector when the question is “Should I live here?” You get a BnBIndex from 0 to 100 and the count of active Airbnb listings nearby, about an 80-meter radius, usually in under 10 seconds.

Choose AirDNA when the question is “What could this place earn as a short-term rental, and how does this market behave?” AirDNA’s public product is market research, revenue and occupancy estimates, comparable listings, and pricing tools for hosts and property managers. AirDNA describes its data as covering major booking sites, not Airbnb alone, and it publishes a free tier plus paid research plans. Current packages: https://www.airdna.co/pricing

A building can look excellent in a revenue model and poor as a home. Those are consistent results, not a contradiction.

## What each product is organized around

### BnBDetector

- One address, one living decision.
- Nearby active Airbnb count and a 0–100 score with plain-language bands.
- Same report on Starter, Max, and Ultra. Plans change volume and tools such as reCheck and history.
- Private to the account. The building is not notified.
- No revenue forecast, no occupancy rate, no nightly price recommendation.
- Published entry price is a small report pack for people checking a shortlist, not a professional data terminal. See https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing

### AirDNA

- Markets, comps, and property-level revenue potential for people who host or invest.
- Public materials describe historical performance, future demand, and dynamic pricing (Adapt) that can sync rates to a listing or a property-management system.
- Built for operators. The questions on the site are about ADR, occupancy, and revenue.
- Broader booking-site coverage than BnBDetector’s Airbnb count, which matters when you are estimating a host’s competitive set.
- Check AirDNA’s own pricing page before you compare cost. The plans are aimed at ongoing market research, and they are a different purchase from a handful of address checks.

## Can one replace the other?

No. A BnBIndex of 80 does not tell you annual revenue. An AirDNA revenue estimate does not tell you whether you want to raise a family in that hallway. If you are buying with the intent to host, you may look at both, and you still have to read the license rules. BnBDetector will not confirm that hosting is legal. AirDNA’s regulatory notes, where a specific AirDNA workflow includes them, are AirDNA’s product, not a substitute for the city’s own registry.

## Privacy and audience

BnBDetector’s audience is the person who will sleep there, plus agents screening on that person’s behalf. Searches stay on the user’s account.

AirDNA’s audience is the supply side: hosts, managers, and investors researching performance. Read AirDNA’s own privacy and data terms before you paste client addresses into a revenue calculator. This page does not summarize those terms.

## A clean split of work

1. If the goal is to live there, run the address on BnBDetector and read the band. Prefer 0–30 for a residential building. Treat 59–100 as heavy listing pressure.
2. If the goal is to operate a short-term rental, use a host-analytics product and the local permit rules.
3. If you are an agent serving both kinds of clients, label which question the attachment answers. Do not send a revenue printout to a family that asked whether the building is quiet, and do not send a BnBIndex to an investor as a pro forma.

## Bottom line

AirDNA answers “How does this short-term rental market perform?” BnBDetector answers “How much Airbnb activity is around this address for someone who has to live with it?”

Pick the question first.
