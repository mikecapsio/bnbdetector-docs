# BnBDetector vs Inside Airbnb

Inside Airbnb is a public data project. BnBDetector is a private address check. They meet because both are about Airbnb’s effect on housing, and they diverge immediately after that.

Use Inside Airbnb when you want maps and downloadable data for a city they cover, for research, journalism, advocacy, or policy. Use BnBDetector when you are about to rent or buy a specific building and you want a BnBIndex for that address.

## Short answer

Inside Airbnb, founded by Murray Cox and stewarded with the Housing Justice Data Lab, collects publicly visible Airbnb information and publishes it so communities can see how short-term rentals affect housing. You explore cities they have added, and you can download listings, calendars, reviews, and neighborhood files where they offer them. Coverage is those published places, including country archives they list, not a box that accepts every street address on earth. Start at https://insideairbnb.com.

BnBDetector takes an address you type, counts active Airbnb listings nearby (about 80 meters in the current product), and returns a 0–100 BnBIndex, a category, and a short verdict. The report is saved to your account. It is not a public dataset, and the building is not notified. Most checks finish in under 10 seconds. https://www.bnbdetector.com

## What you can and cannot do with each

### Inside Airbnb

- See city and regional patterns: where listings cluster, how many are entire homes, how the public files describe the market.
- Download data for analysis, subject to the project’s own terms and the limits of what they publish.
- Cite a public source in a story or a campaign. That is the point of the project.
- You cannot assume your city is there. If it is not in their list, there is no file to open.
- You do not get a personal “should I sign this lease” score for an arbitrary building as a product workflow. You would be doing your own spatial analysis on their files, where the files exist, and those files have their own collection date.

### BnBDetector

- Check the building on your shortlist, including cities that have no Inside Airbnb page.
- Compare several addresses on one scale the same day.
- Keep the search off the public internet. There is no public map of what you looked up.
- Refresh a saved address with reCheck on Max and Ultra, once per address each month.
- You do not get a citywide dataset, a download for researchers, or an advocacy map. The report is one address at a time, and it is a paid report after the free account.
- You should not describe a BnBIndex as “Inside Airbnb says” or as an official open dataset. It is a BnBDetector report.

## Timing and geography

Inside Airbnb’s value is depth on the places volunteers and partners have brought in, with a collection date you can read in their data notes. A file from a public dataset is the wrong object to treat as this week’s reading of one lobby.

BnBDetector’s value is a current check on the address in front of you. It will not draw you a map of the whole city. The site’s city guides are written background, not a dump of listings: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/guides/cities

The search radius is also different in spirit. A city extract is a city. A BnBDetector count is the immediate surroundings of one address. Quote the one you actually used.

## Who should use which

- A renter, buyer, nomad, or family choosing a home: BnBDetector, then a visit.
- A journalist or researcher describing a city’s Airbnb market: Inside Airbnb, where they publish that city, plus whatever local registry exists.
- A neighbor writing to a board about one building: a dated BnBDetector report for that address, plus your own incident log. A city map is context. The address report is the relevant attachment.
- A planner who needs both the pattern and a spot check can use both, and should not merge their numbers into one statistic. They were collected differently.

## Price and access

Inside Airbnb’s published data is a public project. Read their site for how they want the files used.

BnBDetector is an account product. Published plans start at $19 for 10 reports. The pricing page is the authority: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing

## Bottom line

Inside Airbnb answers “What does the public Airbnb data show for this city?” BnBDetector answers “What Airbnb activity is detectable around this address, for my decision this week?”

They sit well side by side. They are not substitutes.
