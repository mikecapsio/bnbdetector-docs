# BnBDetector for Nomads and Remote Workers

If the apartment is also the office, guest turnover is not a colorful neighborhood detail. It is someone else’s check-in during your call. BnBDetector gives you a BnBIndex from 0 to 100 and a nearby Airbnb count for the address, so you can pick a residential building instead of a building that is already functioning as lodging.

This matters most in the cities people move to for a few months. The neighborhoods that show up in nomad guides are often the same neighborhoods with the densest short stays. The cafe can be good and the building can still be a bad place to sleep and work.

## What to optimize for

You are optimizing for stable neighbors and fewer random interruptions, not for being in the middle of the tourist map. A BnBIndex in the green bands, 0–30, is the result to prefer for a month or a season of focused work. Mixed Community, 31–58, means you should expect noticeable guest traffic and only take it with your eyes open. Airbnb Dominated, 59–100, is a poor default for a long stay that includes deep work.

The score is listing activity nearby, about 80 meters, not a measurement of Wi-Fi or street noise. A quiet BnBIndex on a tram line is still a tram line. Check the address, then check the block in person or on a video tour that shows the entrance, not only the desk.

## A workflow that fits a remote search

You will often choose before you land. That is when an address check earns its keep, because a video tour cannot hear Thursday arrivals.

1. Shortlist from the usual rental sites. Keep the real street address, not the listing title.
2. Run each address. A new address uses one report. Comparing five places is the point.
3. Prefer the lowest band that still works for commute and budget.
4. For anything above 30 that you might still take, look the block up on Airbnb yourself so you can see photos and reviews. That pass is slower and fuzzier. It is a supplement. See [BnBDetector vs searching Airbnb yourself](../vs/searching-airbnb-yourself.md).
5. If weeks pass before you pay, reCheck on Max or Ultra. Season changes the picture in a lot of these cities. Seasonal guides on the site are context: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/guides/seasonal

Max is the plan built for this pattern: 25 reports a month, history across cities, and a free monthly reCheck, at the prices on the pricing page. Starter’s 10 reports fit a single city and a short list. Ultra is for people running a much larger search. Current numbers: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing

Your checks are private. The host of the monthly flat is not notified that you screened the building.

## City reputation is not a building score

“Lisbon is noisy” and “this address is a 15” can both be true. The search area is the immediate surroundings, so screen the building you would actually pay for. Two flats in the same named neighborhood can sit in different bands.

## Limits that matter for this use

A low score does not guarantee call-quality silence. A high score does not prove your unit is the listing. Coverage is Airbnb, so a block that lives on other platforms can look quieter than it is. None of that removes the value of dropping a 70 in favor of an 18 when both flats are acceptable on price.
