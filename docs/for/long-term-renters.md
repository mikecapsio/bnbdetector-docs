# BnBDetector for Long-Term Renters

A long lease is a bet that the building will still feel like a home in month eight. Photos and a weekday tour do not show suitcase traffic, weekend guests, or a lobby that never has the same faces. BnBDetector is the check you run on the address before that bet is locked in.

You get a BnBIndex from 0 to 100 and the count of active Airbnb listings nearby, usually in under 10 seconds. Lower is the quieter residential signal. The search stays on your account. The landlord is not told you looked.

## What you are trying to avoid

Short stays and long leases want opposite things from a hallway. You want the same neighbors and predictable nights. Guests are on a trip. In a building with heavy Airbnb activity that shows up as late arrivals, weekend noise, shared codes handed to strangers, and common areas that get used like a hotel. You discover it after the deposit only if you skip the check.

The score does not promise silence. It tells you whether Airbnb listings are already clustered around that address.

## How to use it while you search

1. Build the shortlist the way you already do: budget, commute, size, light.
2. Run each serious address, not only the one you like in photos. Each new address uses one report.
3. Read the nearby count and the BnBIndex together. The bands:
   - **0–30 (Local Haven or Residential Core).** Prefer these when you have a choice.
   - **31–58 (Mixed Community).** You will likely notice guest traffic. Keep it only if the rest of the flat is clearly better and you have seen the building at a busy hour.
   - **59–100 (Airbnb Dominated).** Keep looking if a stable home is the point of the lease.
4. Visit the finalists. A Tuesday morning tour is a weak sample. Friday evening tells you more.
5. Read the clause on subletting and minimum stay. A low score plus a written ban is a stronger position than either one alone.
6. On Max or Ultra, reCheck the address you are about to sign. Listings change during a long search. reCheck is free once per address each month.

Starter ($19 once, 10 reports, at the prices published with this guide) covers a normal shortlist for one move. Max ($29/month, 25 reports) fits a search that sprawls across neighborhoods or months, and it adds history plus reCheck. Confirm prices at https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing.

## How to compare two flats you both like

Ignore a two-point gap inside the same band. Pay attention when the category changes. A 24 and a 63 are not “a bit busier.” One still reads as residential. The other reads as a tourist building.

If the scores are close, use the listing counts, then use everything BnBDetector does not know: the floor plan, the street itself, the landlord, and the rent.

## What the report will not do

It will not name the unit next door, measure decibels, or tell you the lease is enforceable. It counts Airbnb listings in a small area, about 80 meters, so a busy building across the alley can affect the number. It does not include a listing that exists only on another site. Details: [How to read a BnBIndex report](../how-to-read-a-report.md).

## A first session

1. Open the example so you know the layout: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/reports/example
2. Sign in with Google and pick Starter or Max.
3. Check three addresses you are actually considering.
4. Drop the Airbnb Dominated ones unless you have a reason to live with that.
5. Book viewings for the low scores, and look at the common areas on purpose.
