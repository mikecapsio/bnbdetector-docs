# BnBDetector for Neighbors Already in the Building

If you already live there, you do not need a detector to hear a Saturday party. You need a way to separate a bad week from a building that has a lot of Airbnb listings around it, and a dated note you can show a manager without claiming more than the data says.

BnBDetector checks the address and returns a BnBIndex from 0 to 100 plus the count of active Airbnb listings nearby. The area is small, about 80 meters. The report does not name the unit, the host, or the listing. Your search is not sent to the landlord.

## What you can learn

- Whether detected Airbnb activity around your address is light (0–30), mixed (31–58), or heavy (59–100).
- A count you can compare with the count next month, using reCheck on Max or Ultra once per address each month.
- A difference between your building’s immediate area and another building you might move to. Sometimes the useful output is “I should leave,” not “I should complain.”

Visible clues still matter, and they answer a different question. Rotating luggage, a lockbox, cleaners between stays, and weekend-only noise suggest a specific flat. The score suggests the area is thick with listings. Use both. Neither one is a court judgment.

## How to use a report if you write to management

Include:

- The address you checked.
- The date.
- The BnBIndex, the category, and the nearby count.
- Your own log: dates, times, and what happened. The report does not contain that log.

Leave out:

- Claims that a named apartment is the listing, unless you have a separate basis for that.
- Claims that the city has already found a violation.
- Claims that BnBDetector measured the noise.

Ask for the rule that applies: the lease, the house rules, the minimum stay, the city’s registration scheme. A high score makes the conversation concrete. The rule is what management can enforce. Background on tenant-rights themes by city: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/guides/tenant-rights

BnBDetector will not contact the host, the platform, or the city for you.

## If you are deciding whether to renew

Treat renewal like a new lease. ReCheck, or run the address again, and run two or three alternative buildings. A score you remember from move-in day is not the score now. The [long-term renter guide](long-term-renters.md) is the comparison workflow. The [reading guide](../how-to-read-a-report.md) is how to quote the number without overstating it.

## A restraint worth keeping

A report is a poor tool for confronting a neighbor in the hallway. It is a reasonable attachment to a written note to management, next to your own dates and the building’s rules. If the situation is a safety issue happening now, use the local emergency or non-emergency number. Do not wait for a score.
