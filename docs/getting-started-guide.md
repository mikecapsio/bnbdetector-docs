# BnBDetector Getting Started Guide

BnBDetector checks Airbnb activity around an address before you commit to living there. You enter the building, get a BnBIndex from 0 to 100, and see how many active Airbnb listings were detected nearby. Lower is the quieter residential signal.

The basic workflow is:

1. Create an account and pick a plan.
2. Enter the address.
3. Read the score and the nearby count together.
4. Repeat for the rest of the shortlist.
5. Refresh the final address before you sign, if your plan includes reCheck.
6. Still read the lease, visit if you can, and check local rules.

## Before you start

You need:

- A Google account for sign-in. Creating a BnBDetector account is free. You must be 18 or older.
- A plan with reports left. Starter is a one-time pack. Max and Ultra are monthly.
- The full street address, not only a neighborhood name. Suggestions appear as you type. Pick the one that matches the building.
- A short list if you are choosing between places. One score is useful. Three scores are a comparison.

Most reports finish in under 10 seconds.

Open the example first if you want to see the layout with no report spent: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/reports/example

## Step 1: Create an account

Go to https://www.bnbdetector.com and sign in with Google. The account holds your reports and your plan. You can look around before you pay. A check spends a report, so choose a plan when you are ready to run addresses.

## Step 2: Choose a plan

The report contents do not change by plan. You always get the BnBIndex, the nearby Airbnb count, the category, the verdict, and the activity scale.

Starter fits a single housing search. Max fits ongoing travel, a family comparing neighborhoods, or a relocation with more than a handful of buildings. Ultra fits someone checking dozens of addresses a month. Teams that need more than that, several seats, or a custom setup should use https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/contact.

Confirm the current numbers on https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/pricing before you buy. Subscriptions renew until you cancel. Starter is not a subscription.

## Step 3: Enter the address

From the dashboard, type the address and choose the suggestion that matches the building. A neighborhood, a mall, or a city name will not tell you about one building.

If the address cannot be located, try the form that appears in the suggestions: street, number, city, and country. A score needs a place the product can pin.

Each new address uses one report from your balance. Running the same address again soon may return the recent result without using another report. When you need a fresh read of an address you already saved, use reCheck on Max or Ultra.

## Step 4: Read the report

Look at five things, in this order:

1. **Airbnbs nearby.** This is the count of active Airbnb listings detected around the address, in a small area of about 80 meters. It is the immediate surroundings, not the whole district and not a list of apartment numbers.
2. **BnBIndex.** A 0–100 summary of that nearby activity. Lower is quieter.
3. **Category.** Local Haven (0–9), Residential Core (10–30), Mixed Community (31–58), or Airbnb Dominated (59–100).
4. **Verdict.** One sentence that says what that band means for daily life.
5. **Activity scale.** Where this address sits between Quiet and Airbnb heavy.

A worked reading guide is in [How to read a BnBIndex report](how-to-read-a-report.md).

Practical cut for most people who want a residential building:

- 0–30 is the range to prefer when you have a choice.
- 31–58 means you will likely notice guest traffic. Decide with your eyes open, and compare it with a quieter option if you have one.
- 59–100 is a strong reason to keep looking if a quiet home is the goal.

Those cutoffs are decision aids, not house rules. A buyer in a resort town and a student who needs the cheapest room near campus will weigh the same number differently.

## Step 5: Compare the shortlist

Run every serious option, not only the favorite. The point of a shared scale is that “this one is a 12 and that one is a 71” is easier to act on than a vague sense from a Sunday tour.

Keep notes outside the tool as well: floor, price, commute, light, and what you saw in the lobby. BnBDetector covers Airbnb pressure. It does not cover the rest of the apartment.

On Max and Ultra, past reports stay in the dashboard, including views by city and country. That matters when a search spans more than one place. Starter is built to check the list in front of you, without that library.

## Step 6: ReCheck before you commit

Airbnb listings appear and disappear. A score from the start of a search can be wrong by the day you transfer a deposit.

Max and Ultra include reCheck: open a saved address and refresh it once per month without spending a report. Do that on the final building in the last days before you sign or close.

Starter does not include reCheck. If the first check is old and the decision is now, run the address again if you still have reports, or move to a plan that includes reCheck.

## Step 7: Do the checks a score cannot do

Before you commit:

- Visit at a noisy hour if you can: Friday evening, Sunday morning, school-holiday weekends. Lockboxes, rolling luggage, and a lobby that feels like a check-in desk are visible clues.
- Read the lease or the building rules for subletting, minimum stay, and short-term rental bans.
- Ask what the building actually enforces. A written ban and a hallway full of guests can both be true.
- Look up the city’s own rules if legality matters. BnBDetector does not check permits. Regulation guides on the site are background, not a legal opinion: https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/guides/regulations
- If you are buying, read the HOA or condo documents yourself. Courts and building rules live there, not in the BnBIndex.

The report is one input. The Terms of Service describe it as informational and provided as-is.

## What you are paying to see

You are paying for a fast, private, comparable read of nearby Airbnb listings. You are not paying for:

- Proof that a specific unit is listed.
- A noise measurement.
- A legal determination.
- A valuation or a rent forecast.
- Coverage of every booking site. The count is Airbnb. A listing that exists only elsewhere can be missing.
- A notice to the landlord. Your search stays on your account.

Details and the privacy boundaries are in the [FAQ](faq.md).

## Common problems

### The suggestion list does not show my building

Add the street number, city, and country. Abbreviated or translated street names sometimes need a second try. Pick a suggestion rather than submitting a free-typed string that never resolves.

### The score is low and the building still feels busy

The score is Airbnb activity nearby, not all human noise. Construction, a bar downstairs, thin floors, and listings on other platforms will not show up as Airbnb counts. Walk the building.

### The score is high and the apartment itself seemed empty

You may have visited between guests. A high nearby count means listing activity around the address, which can be this building or the buildings immediately around it. Look at the count, then look at the block.

### I checked the neighborhood name and got a confusing result

Check the street address. A district score is not a building score. The search area is about 80 meters, so two buildings on the same street can differ.

### I am out of reports

Upgrade from the dashboard if you are on Max or Ultra, or buy Starter if you only need a small pack. Custom volume is a contact-page conversation.

### I need this for a client or a team

Use your own account for checks you run. Do not share the login. For several seats or more than Ultra’s monthly allowance, write to the team from https://www.bnbdetector.com/en/contact.
